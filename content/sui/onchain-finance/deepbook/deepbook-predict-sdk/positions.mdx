The position functions in the DeepBook Predict SDK place and follow queued orders, quote a mint, claim a position held in the account, list the positions an account holds, and decode the receipt of each trade. [Predict](/onchain-finance/deepbook/deepbook-predict/contract-information/predict) explains the mechanics that these builders wrap.

Both networks run Predict protocol version 4, where every trade is a queued order, so use the functions under [Queued orders](#queued-orders). Version 2.7.0 records the Testnet deployment, and Mainnet needs version `TBD-DBU-890: SDK release` or later. `read.executionMode()` confirms which path a network is on.

Every function is a member of `client.predict`, which [DeepBook Predict SDK](/onchain-finance/deepbook/deepbook-predict-sdk#client) shows how to register. Each builder and quote that targets a market takes the market as a [market descriptor](/onchain-finance/deepbook/deepbook-predict-sdk/markets): an object that names the market by its `underlying`, `expiryMs`, and optional `marketId`, plus the strike, as a binary or range position, where the call needs one. The function then reads the chain to resolve the market object and caches it per client, so those functions are asynchronous. The SDK takes and returns amounts as decimal USDC values and converts them to 6-decimal base units; see [Units](/onchain-finance/deepbook/deepbook-predict-sdk#units).

## Queued orders {#queued-orders}

From Predict protocol version 4, a mint or an early sell is a queued order. You place it with its limits and its money, it waits in its market's queue until Pyth Lazer publishes its signed price for the order's price instant, τ, about 800 ms after placement, and a keeper or any other caller then fills it at that price or refunds it. Version 2.7.0 of the SDK adds the queued path to `client.predict` and records Testnet's delayed-execution deployment, and version `TBD-DBU-890: SDK release` records Mainnet's. With an older release on Mainnet, the queued functions throw `PredictInputError` unless you pass a `config` that sets `packages.predictDelayedExecution`, `packages.predictOrders`, `objects.orderDesk`, and `objects.queueRegistry`.

A queued order differs from an immediate trade in 4 ways:

- **Placement and fill are separate transactions.** The enqueue debits the order's budget and a flat order fee into the order's escrow and returns a record ID. The fill lands about a second later in another caller's transaction. Unused budget comes back at the fill.
- **The slippage is cents per contract.** `slippageCents` bounds how far the all-in price per `$1` of payout may rise above the quote's price without the fee subsidy, so `slippageCents: 10` lets a contract quoted at 42 cents before the subsidy fill at up to 52 cents, fees included. It is never a percentage. `'auto'` sizes it from a model of the price's movement until τ.
- **A filled mint is an Open record.** It stays in the market's queue rather than entering the account, so `read.positions` does not list it. Follow it by its record ID, sell it with another queued order, and let the queue's settlement walk pay it after expiry.
- **A refund is a normal outcome.** An order that misses its limits at τ, or that the market cannot cover, is refunded with a reason code, and a refund for the order's own limits or for admission keeps the order fee.

### `read.executionMode` {#execution-mode}

Use `read.executionMode` to find out which trade path a network is on. It returns a `Promise<ExecutionMode>`, one of `'immediate'`, `'awaiting-cutover'`, `'delayed'`, `'unsupported'`, or `'retired'`. It reads the `ProtocolConfig` version watermark and checks whether the configuration records delayed execution. `'retired'` means a later upgrade raised a version floor above the code this SDK release calls, so upgrade the SDK.

### `read.planMint` {#plan-mint}

Use `read.planMint` to build a purchase form's state and the limits a queued mint carries. It returns a `Promise<MintPlan>`. It quotes the order at the current price, for a visitor without an account too, turns the slippage into the enqueue's limits, and runs the enqueue builder's preflight, so `accepting` is `false`, with a typed `refusal`, when the order would be refused now. The preflight covers the queue gates, the balance, and the order's limits, not every onchain check.

It takes these parameters:

- `owner`: The address that owns the account.
- `m`: The market and strike, as a binary or range market descriptor. Snap a numeric strike onto the admission grid with `snapStrike`, and prefer a strike near the money, because admission refuses an entry probability outside the market's band.
- `opts`: A `PlanMintOptions` object: either `amount`, an all-in spend in USDC that includes the order fee unless `orderFee` is `'exclusive'`, or `quantity`, an exact payout, plus `slippageCents`.

<ImportContent source="packages/deepbook-v3/src/predict/client.ts" mode="code" org="MystenLabs" repo="ts-sdks" branch="26f6fdfae9604d08f27ebe150f1533fb95800422" lines="2459-2463" />

A `MintPlan` carries `potentialPayout`, `minPayout` at the worst price, `payoutMultiple`, `pricePerContract`, `worstPricePerContract`, `orderFee`, `budget`, `totalDebit`, the account's `balance`, `maxNow`, the largest amount the market and the balance take now, a `timing` preview of τ and the deadline, and the `order` the plan builds. The limits are sized from the price without the fee subsidy, because placement checks the order without it. `planMint` throws a `PredictPreflightError` with code `entry-band` for a strike outside the band, `min-premium` for a budget that buys less than 1 USDC of premium, and `past-cutoff` for a market past its expiry, where the chain has no live price to quote. Near the cutoff, the plan's `refusal` is `past-cutoff` instead, and the preview runs on the device clock, so the chain can still refuse an order placed in the last seconds.

### `tx.enqueuePlan` {#enqueue-plan}

Use `tx.enqueuePlan` to queue the order a plan describes. It returns a `Promise<QueuedOrderPlan>`, the `transaction` and a `preview` of τ, the deadline, the escrow, the order fee, and whether a sell needs funding. Pass the descriptor the mint plan was planned with, or the coordinates a sell plan took. It reads the gates and the balance again and throws a `PredictPreflightError` for a refused plan or a balance that now escrows less than the plan's budget, and a `PredictInputError` for an order fee that rose since the plan or for another owner, market, strike, or record than the plan's target. Each enqueue it builds starts with a check that aborts the transaction if the order desk's fee rose between building and execution.

<ImportContent source="packages/deepbook-v3/src/predict/client.ts" mode="code" org="MystenLabs" repo="ts-sdks" branch="26f6fdfae9604d08f27ebe150f1533fb95800422" lines="1934-1938" />

The lower-level builders `tx.enqueueMint`, `tx.enqueueMintAmount`, `tx.enqueueMintCost`, and `tx.enqueueSell` take the limits directly, in SDK units, and run the same preflight. Every queued mint needs a finite cost cap, because the chain aborts a zero or unlimited `max_cost` with `EMintCostCapRequired`.

The preflight codes are `not-live`, `no-queue`, `retired`, `paused`, `stuck`, `past-cutoff`, `queue-full`, `account-cap`, `fee`, `market-cash`, `min-premium`, `entry-band`, `cost-above-payout`, `below-min-sell`, `record-not-open`, and `not-record-owner`. `fee` means the balance cannot cover the order fee, or the plan's whole debit, so prompt a deposit. Some checks run only on chain at placement, such as the market's SVI age and room in the payout tree for a new strike, and those abort the transaction rather than raising a preflight code. `describePredictError` turns any of these aborts into readable text.

### `decode.enqueue` {#decode-enqueue}

Use `decode.enqueue` to read the record ID, τ, deadline, escrow, and order fee from the enqueue's `OrderEnqueued` event. It throws unless the result holds exactly one, and `decode.enqueues` returns every enqueue in a batched transaction. Persist `recordId`: it is the handle for everything that follows, and nothing onchain lists an account's queue records for you.

### `read.waitForOutcome`, `read.order`, and `read.orders` {#wait-for-outcome}

Use `read.waitForOutcome` to poll a record until it is filled, refunded, or closed. It returns a `Promise<QueuedOrderOutcome>` whose `outcome` is `'filled'`, `'refunded'`, `'closed'`, `'gone'`, or `'timeout'`, with the record's `order` view. It polls every 250 ms by default for up to 30 seconds, and keeps polling a record the full node does not show yet. After expiry, `'gone'` for a filled record means the queue paid it out and deleted it, not that the order was lost.

<ImportContent source="packages/deepbook-v3/src/predict/client.ts" mode="code" org="MystenLabs" repo="ts-sdks" branch="26f6fdfae9604d08f27ebe150f1533fb95800422" lines="2872-2876" />

`read.order(m, recordId)` and `read.orders(m, recordIds)` read records without waiting. Each record's `view.state` is `'placed'`, `'priced'`, `'refunding'`, `'filled'`, `'refunded'`, or `'closed'`. A filled record's `view.position` carries the packed `orderId` and the held quantity, and `view.sellable` reports whether it can be sold now. A keeper that commits a price and fills in one transaction moves an order from `'placed'` straight to `'filled'`, so do not count on seeing `'priced'`.

### `read.planSell` {#plan-sell}

Use `read.planSell` to plan an early sell of an Open record. It returns a `Promise<SellPlan>` with the `proceeds` at the current price, `net` after the order fee, the `minProceeds` and `minNet` floors at the worst price the slippage allows, and the `order` for `tx.enqueuePlan`. It throws `PredictPreflightError` with code `record-not-open` for a record that is not Open. A sell pays the order fee whatever it returns, so `net` is negative for a sell worth less than the fee. `read.quoteSell` returns the same quote without the plan.

It takes these parameters:

- `owner`: The address that owns the account and the record.
- `m`: The market's `underlying`, `expiryMs`, and optional `marketId`.
- `opts`: A `PlanSellOptions` object with the `recordId`, the payout `quantity` to sell, and `slippageCents`.

<ImportContent source="packages/deepbook-v3/src/predict/client.ts" mode="code" org="MystenLabs" repo="ts-sdks" branch="26f6fdfae9604d08f27ebe150f1533fb95800422" lines="2762-2766" />

The whole position moves into the sell's own record, so after a sell, including a partial one, the remainder lives under the sell's record ID. A sell the market cannot cover at the fill is refunded and the position returns to an Open record. When a sell's cash need is above the market's spare cash, `tx.enqueueSell` appends `rebalance_expiry_cash` after the enqueue by default, and its `fundMarket` option controls that.

### `tx.refund`, `tx.claimParked`, and `tx.payOpen` {#refund}

Keepers refund an order still unfinished 5 seconds after its τ, so these builders are recovery paths, and each is permissionless:

- **`tx.refund(m, { maxOrders })`** refunds the market's orders past their deadline. Offer it only when a record's `view.canRequestRefund` is `true`, and never prepend it to other transactions. It needs no account and works during the emergency freeze.
- **`tx.claimParked(m, recordId)`** sends a finished record's parked USDC, a refund or change its receive address could not take while denied, to that address once it is clear.
- **`tx.payOpen(m, recordId)`** pays a settled Open record that the settlement walk skipped.

### `read.settlement` and `cost.settledPayout` {#settlement}

After expiry, `read.market`, `read.price`, and the quotes abort with `ELivePricingExpired`, and the queue's keeper settles the market, pays each Open record, and deletes it, usually within seconds. `read.settlement(m)` still works: it returns the settlement price as a decimal and as `settlementPriceRaw`, with `tickSizeRaw`, or `settled: false` until a keeper settles. Pass the stored `orderId` with those 2 raw values to `cost.settledPayout` for what the position is owed at that price, in raw USDC. That is a calculation, not proof of payment: the payout walk's `OpenRecordSettled` event is the payment to the account's receive address, and a record the walk skipped, because the market was short of cash or the address was denied, emits `OpenRecordPayoutSkipped` instead and stays Open until `tx.payOpen` pays it. `read.pendingFunds(owner)` reports refunds, sell proceeds, and payouts that reached the account but are not settled into its stored balance yet, which `read.balance` already counts.

### Queue decoders {#queue-decoders}

`decode.queueEvents` returns every queue event in a result, tagged and in chain order, and `queue.reduceOrderEvents` folds a stream of them into the same order states the reads return, for an indexer or an event feed. The typed decoders are `decode.cohortCommits`, `decode.queuedFills`, `decode.queuedRefunds`, `decode.openRecordPayouts`, `decode.marketPayoutsCompleted`, `decode.recordFunds`, and `decode.queueOps`. They match the order-flow package's events against its original ID, and `decode.queuedFills` sits beside `decode.mints` and `decode.redeems`, which still read the Predict `OrderMinted` and `LiveOrderRedeemed` that every fill emits.

## Quotes {#mint-functions}

`read.quoteMint` and `read.quoteMintCost` quote a mint without placing it. They read Predict's protocol version 4 quote functions and preview a queued fill at the current clock: the quote has `queued: true`, `fees.penalty` is 0, and the flat order fee is in `orderFee`, on top of `cost`. The fill itself prices at its committed Pyth tick, so a quote is an estimate. For an order form, [`read.planMint`](#plan-mint) is the fuller tool, because it adds the slippage limits and the enqueue builder's preflight. Both quotes read the owner's account, so it must exist, and both throw `PredictInputError` when the configuration lacks the order-flow package or its desk. Neither checks that the balance covers the order, so use a plan or the enqueue preflight for that.

### `read.quoteMint` {#quote-mint}

Use `read.quoteMint` to quote an exact payout quantity. It returns a `Promise<MintQuote>`, priced through `quote_mint_for_account`, so the quote carries the account's builder fee. An exact-quantity quote is not capped by the account's balance. It takes the `owner`, the market descriptor `m`, and an object with the payout `quantity`, a whole lot of 0.01 USDC.

<ImportContent source="packages/deepbook-v3/src/predict/client.ts" mode="code" org="MystenLabs" repo="ts-sdks" branch="26f6fdfae9604d08f27ebe150f1533fb95800422" lines="2261-2265" />

`MintQuote` carries `entryProbability`, `premium`, a `fees` breakdown of `trading`, `subsidy`, `builder`, `penalty`, `referral`, and `inventoryImpact`, the all-in `cost`, the `quantity`, a `raw` block of exact `bigint` amounts, and, for a queued preview, `queued` and `orderFee`. `cost` is `premium + (trading - subsidy) + builder + penalty + inventoryImpact`, and `referral` is a part of the trading fee that the protocol routes to a referrer, not an extra debit.

### `read.quoteMintCost` {#quote-mint-cost}

Use `read.quoteMintCost` to see what an all-in budget buys. It returns a `Promise<MintQuote>`, priced through `quote_mint_exact_cost_for_account` at `min(spend, available - orderFee)`, the budget an enqueue escrows, so this quote is capped by the account's balance. It takes the `owner`, the market descriptor `m`, and an object with `spend` and `minQuantity`. Read `quantity` from it to set a `minQuantity` floor on the queued order.

### Deprecated immediate builders {#immediate-builders}

Before protocol version 4, `tx.mint`, `tx.mintAmount`, and `tx.mintCost` built the immediate mints, `tx.redeem` built the live close, and `read.quoteRedeem` quoted it. Protocol version 4 always aborts those calls, with `EDelayedExecutionRequired`, on both networks, and version 2.7.0 deprecates the 5 functions. Build every trade with [`tx.enqueuePlan`](#enqueue-plan) or the `enqueue*` builders instead, and quote an early sell with `read.planSell` or `read.quoteSell`.

### `tx.claimSettled` {#claim-settled}

Use `tx.claimSettled` to claim a position held in the account, which only an immediate mint from before the cutover created, once its market has settled. It is the SDK form of the owner-authorized `redeem_settled` and returns a `Promise<Transaction>`. A settled claim always closes the order in full, so the builder takes no quantity, and the order ID identifies the position, so it takes no side or strike. The payout is the full quantity when the settlement price lands inside the position's range and 0 otherwise. It does not pay a queued fill, which the queue's settlement walk pays instead.

Before settlement the claim aborts with `EMarketNotSettled`, and expiry alone does not settle a market. Settlement runs through the permissionless `expiry_market::try_settle`, which the SDK does not build. The SDK also builds no `redeem_settled_permissionless`, which only allowlisted keepers can call.

It takes these parameters:

- `owner`: The address that owns the account and signs the transaction.
- `m`: The market's `underlying`, `expiryMs`, and optional `marketId`.
- `opts`: An object with the position's `orderId`.

## Position reads {#position-reads}

The SDK reads positions straight from the account's onchain position table, so neither read needs an indexer. The fastest path is to persist the order ID from each mint receipt and replace it after each partial close; use these reads to recover a lost ID or to validate a stored one.

### `read.positions` {#positions}

Use `read.positions` to list every open position an owner's account holds. A queued fill is not one of them: it is an Open record in the market's queue, which `read.order` reads. It returns a `Promise<OpenPosition[]>`, an entry for each position, each carrying the `marketId` and `orderId` that `tx.redeem`, `tx.claimSettled`, and `read.hasPosition` take. It returns an empty array for an owner with no Predict account or no Predict positions.

The SDK caches the account and table IDs per owner once the table exists, so later reads cost 1 request per page of positions. A transport failure throws rather than reading as an empty list, and a table that still has entries after 10 pages throws a plain `Error`. `OpenPosition` carries no underlying or expiry, so supply those yourself when you build the descriptor for a close.

It takes this parameter:

- `owner`: The address that owns the account.

### `read.hasPosition` {#has-position}

Use `read.hasPosition` to check whether an owner's account still holds a specific order on a specific market. It returns a `Promise<boolean>`. It is the cheap check for an order ID your app stored, which goes stale after a full close or after a partial close replaces it. The read loads the owner's wrapper, so call it for an account that exists.

It takes these parameters:

- `owner`: The address that owns the account.
- `marketId`: The object ID of the market's `ExpiryMarket`.
- `orderId`: The order ID to check, as a `bigint`.

## Receipt decoders {#decode}

The receipt decoders turn an executed transaction's events into typed receipts, with no network call. Include events when you execute the transaction, and pass the result. Without events, a singular decoder throws `PredictInputError` because it finds no matching event, and a plural decoder returns an empty array. A decoder also throws `PredictInputError` when a matching event carries no Binary Canonical Serialization (BCS) payload. Each singular decoder throws `PredictInputError` unless the result holds exactly 1 matching event, and each plural decoder returns every matching receipt in event order, for a transaction that batches several trades. [DeepBook Predict SDK](/onchain-finance/deepbook/deepbook-predict-sdk#decode) covers how the decoders read events.

### `decode.mint` and `decode.mints` {#decode-mint}

Use `decode.mint` to read the receipt of a queued mint fill, and `decode.mints` to read every fill in a single transaction, such as a keeper's resolve. Each decodes the [`OrderMinted`](/onchain-finance/deepbook/deepbook-predict/contract-information/predict) event and returns a `MintReceipt`, or an array of them. Keep `orderId`, which `cost.settledPayout` takes to show the position's settled result. `positionRootId` stays stable across partial-close replacements, `quantity` is the quantity the contract actually minted, which it rounds down to a lot for `tx.mintAmount`, and `raw` carries every fee component as an exact `bigint`.

It takes this parameter:

- `r`: The executed transaction result, including its events.

### `decode.redeem` and `decode.redeems` {#decode-redeem}

Use `decode.redeem` to read the receipt of a queued sell fill, and `decode.redeems` to read every sell fill in a single transaction. Each decodes the [`LiveOrderRedeemed`](/onchain-finance/deepbook/deepbook-predict/contract-information/predict) event and returns a `RedeemReceipt`, or an array of them. After a partial close, `replacementOrderId` carries the order ID of the remaining quantity: store it in place of the old one, or the next close targets an order that no longer exists. It is `null` after a full close, and `proceeds` is the net amount the close credits, which the SDK computes the same way as in `RedeemQuote`.

It takes this parameter:

- `r`: The executed transaction result, including its events.

### `decode.claim` and `decode.claims` {#decode-claim}

Use `decode.claim` to read the receipt of a settled claim, and `decode.claims` to read every claim in a single transaction. Each decodes the [`SettledOrderRedeemed`](/onchain-finance/deepbook/deepbook-predict/contract-information/predict) event and returns a `ClaimReceipt`, or an array of them, with the `payout` alongside the market, account, and order identifiers.
